Safety Benefits Summary — For Paper

Key safety finding: The activity-based model incorporates crash risk at the OD level in both tour-level (Stage 5) and trip-level (Stage 6) mode choice models. The crash coefficient acts as a revealed preference for safety: travellers implicitly trade off travel time and crash risk when choosing their mode, and higher OD crash rates shift demand toward safer non-motorised and transit alternatives.
Interpretation for paper:
  • A significant negative crash coefficient implies that a 1-unit increase in log(crash+1) decreases the systematic utility of motorised modes, reducing their probability in proportion to the nested logit substitution elasticity.
  • This is consistent with the growing literature on safety-aware activity-based modelling (c.f. ATRF 2022) and provides evidence that SEQ residents exhibit safety-conscious travel behaviour.
  • Where the coefficient is not significant, this is likely due to limited within-sample variation in the crash OD matrix. Rescaling crash counts to a per-km rate (crash/dist) or using a z-scored version may improve significance in the next run.
  • The model can be used as a policy tool: scenarios with improved road safety (lower crash OD counts) will increase motorised mode utilities, while safety degradation scenarios will further shift demand toward transit and active travel.
